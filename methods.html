Methods

How the datasets were found, how each value was derived, and the rules behind the membrane, orthology and EMC complex calls. Every rule here is applied by the scripts in downloads/code, which rebuild the whole table from the original files (listed under Downloads and code).

Which datasets are included

Only experiments in which an EMC subunit was knocked out or knocked down and the proteome was then measured. Studies of what the EMC binds (co-immunoprecipitation, proximity labelling of the EMC itself) and ribosome profiling are not included.

Datasets were found on 18 Nov 2026 by collecting all 258 papers that cite Shurtleff 2018, Tian 2019 or Volkmar 2019 in Europe PMC, searching their full text for proteomics, perturbation and EMC terms, and running the same search across the rest of Europe PMC to catch papers that cite none of the three. Full-text searching covers open-access papers only; others were screened on title and abstract.

Values

Every value is the log2 change of the perturbed sample over its control. Where the authors give a ratio and a p-value, we use theirs; where they do not, the dataset page says what we calculated and how. Proteins seen in one condition only, or given a floor value instead of a ratio, are shown as present or absent rather than as a number, and nothing is imputed. Values printed as linear fold change (Tang 2017) are converted to log2, and the printed value is kept beside them.

The volcano plots draw reference lines at 1.5-fold and p < 0.05 for comparison across datasets; these are not necessarily the rule each paper used.

Membrane proteins

A protein counts as a membrane protein if it has at least one helical transmembrane segment. Proteins with only a signal peptide, GPI-anchored proteins and beta-barrel proteins (such as VDAC1, VDAC2, TOMM40 and SAMM50) are excluded.

  • Human: a curated list of 4,863 human membrane proteins, matched by UniProt accession first and gene name second.
  • Mouse: the orthologue of a protein on the human list. De Carvalho 2026 also annotate single- and multi-pass proteins themselves; the dataset page shows which source supports each call.
  • Yeast, Xenopus tropicalis, Trypanosoma brucei: UniProt topology for that species. For yeast, UniProt’s curated single- or multi-pass statement agrees with the transmembrane segments for 1,488 of 1,496 proteins. Xenopus and T. brucei entries are mostly predictions.

Orthologues

Each link between a protein from another species and a human membrane protein is supported by at least two independent sources, where possible, and the page shows which sources agree.

  • Mouse: Ensembl, MGI (Alliance) and PANTHER 19. Pairs called by at least two sources are used; a single-source pair is used only when no pair for that human protein reaches two, and is marked.
  • Yeast: Ensembl, PANTHER, OrthoDB and eggNOG. Curated EMC pairs: MMGT1 to EMC5, EMC10 to YDR056C, EMC7 to SOP4.
  • Xenopus tropicalis: Ensembl, PANTHER, Xenbase, OrthoDB and eggNOG. At this distance OrthoDB and eggNOG groups hold whole families, so they count only alongside a pair call.
  • T. brucei: PANTHER, OrthoDB and eggNOG, plus TbEMC5 (Tb927.4.2320) and TbEMC10 (Tb927.1.860) from Iyer 2022.

A pair call comes from a gene-tree or curated orthology source. A family-level match rests only on a shared OrthoDB or eggNOG group, so it marks homology within a family rather than one-to-one orthology. Only proteins on the human membrane list have orthologues mapped; other proteins from non-human datasets can be found under their own names.

EMC complex status

For each condition we look at the change of every EMC subunit that was measured. The complex is called lost when the subunits other than the one perturbed fall 2-fold or more (median log2 change at or below −1). It is called altered when the whole complex does not fall but any subunit, the perturbed one included, falls 2-fold or more, the other subunits fall 1.5-fold or more on average, or the perturbed subunit was knocked out and is not detected. In T. brucei, for example, TbEMC8 knockdown lowers TbEMC3 and TbEMC2 while the other subunits stay flat, so the complex is altered even though most of it remains. It is called intact only when the perturbation cannot be seen in any subunit (for example the EMC4 knockout clones of Tian 2019, where EMC4 itself is only 1.7-fold lower). Where a paper reports no subunits, the state is inferred from the subunit perturbed and marked as inferred. The subunit values are always shown next to the label.

The complex state describes each experiment; it does not enter the ranking score, because losing a single subunit such as EMC4 impairs EMC function without necessarily dismantling the complex.

Consensus ranking

The ranking asks which proteins change most consistently when the EMC is perturbed, across all datasets at once. It uses human proteins: a protein from another species enters through its human orthologue on the membrane list, and proteins from human datasets enter directly.

  • One value per dataset. The conditions of a dataset (clones, tissues, subunits, time points) are averaged, so that no paper counts more than once.
  • Size of change, on a common scale. Each log2 change is divided by the typical spread of its condition (1.4826 × the median absolute deviation over all measured proteins, with a floor of 0.2 so that very tight datasets cannot overstate small changes). A scaled change of 2 therefore means twice the normal scatter of that experiment. Hit lists use a spread of 0.3. A protein seen in one condition only counts as ±4; a protein named as changed without a value counts as ±2. Any single value is capped at ±6.
  • Confidence from the p-value. Each scaled change is multiplied by a confidence of 1 at p ≤ 0.01, 0.5 at p = 0.1 and at least 0.1, rising with −log10 p. Values without a p-value (unreplicated experiments, hit lists, proteins seen in one condition only) get 0.5. The four Volkmar 2019 clones are treated as replicates of one core-subunit loss and tested together. The p-values come from different tests in different papers, so they damp the score rather than define it.
  • Weights. Coverage (full table 1, called hits 0.5, named proteins 0.25) multiplied by orthology confidence for non-human data (a pair supported by two or more sources 1, a single-source pair 0.75, a family-level match 0.5). Species and the state of the complex are not weighted.
  • EMC subunits are ranked like any other protein, except that the perturbed subunit is left out of its own dataset, because its loss is the perturbation rather than a readout.
  • Score. The sum of weight × confident scaled change, divided by the sum of weights plus 2. The extra 2 acts as two full datasets reporting no change, so a protein seen in one dataset cannot outrank one that falls consistently in many. Negative scores mean lower on EMC loss.
  • Two views. All species uses every dataset; Human and mouse uses only those, for proteins whose dependence may not be conserved. The species column shows where a protein clearly changes (scaled change beyond ±2 with p ≤ 0.1 or no p-value): H human, M mouse, X Xenopus, Y yeast, T T. brucei.

The settings are listed in ranking.json with the spread used for every condition, and the full ranking is in ranking.csv.

Topology and the EMC model

Membrane topology comes from the human membrane protein dataset of Wu and Hegde (2026, J Cell Biol): the number of transmembrane domains (TMDs), the length and side of every loop, and whether there is a cleavable signal sequence. The cartoons follow the style of the companion Membrane Protein Topology Viewer: grey membrane, red TMDs, exoplasmic loops in tan above the membrane and cytosolic loops in blue below it, a grey block for the signal sequence, and dotted lines for loops longer than 100 amino acids. Loop heights grow with the logarithm of length, so long loops stay readable.

Each membrane protein is then assigned the most likely step at which the EMC acts, using the current model of EMC insertion (reviewed by Hegde and Keenan, 2024). The first rule that applies is used, and the TMDs it refers to are outlined in dark ink:

  • First TMD, Nexo: no signal sequence, and the first TMD puts its N-terminus in the lumen with an exoplasmic segment of 50 amino acids or less.
  • Final TMD, short exoplasmic tail (or tail anchor for single-pass proteins): the last TMD leaves an exoplasmic C-terminal tail of 50 amino acids or less.
  • First TMD pair: no signal sequence, and TMDs 1 and 2 are joined by an exoplasmic loop of 50 amino acids or less, so the EMC can act when the protein first engages the membrane.
  • Internal pair only: a later pair of TMDs is joined by a short exoplasmic loop, but none of the rules above applies.
  • Not explained: none of the above, for example type I proteins with a signal sequence, or tails just above the 50 amino acid limit.

The 50 amino acid limit is a working cut-off rather than a sharp boundary, so proteins just above it are flagged in the notes. A few proteins carry a manual call instead of the automatic one, marked as such: HSD3B1 and HSD3B2, whose 63 to 64 amino acid C-terminal tails are close enough to the limit to treat them as tail anchors, and TMX2, treated as a pairwise-like insertion of its signal sequence and TMD. Soluble proteins have no cartoon. For the 100 proteins that fall most consistently, the notes give a hand-checked reading: for example, whether a soluble protein is a known partner of an EMC substrate (ATP6V0D1 with the V-ATPase V0 sector) or which datasets drive the change. The topology file is available as data/topology.json.

Downloads and code

  • emc_long.csv: every value, one row per protein per dataset per condition, with its source and rules (column guide).
  • ranking.csv: the consensus score for every protein, with its value in each dataset.
  • conditions.csv: one row per condition, with EMC subunit values and complex status.
  • build.py builds the table from the original files; export_site.py writes the site’s data; datasets_meta.py holds the text of each dataset page.
  • tang2017_tableS2B.csv: Tang 2017 Table S2B, transcribed from the image in the supplement.
  • marquez_uniparc_lookup.py and its output trace obsolete Marquez 2020 accessions through UniProt and UniParc.
